\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\section{Overview}

This thesis studied the design of Bayesian two arm trials with a Normal endpoint that borrow
historical information on the control arm, with the aim of balancing the evidentiary value a
design produces against the number of patients it requires. The vehicle for that balance is the
Experimental Unit Information Index of \citet{held2025balancing}, which normalizes the
\textit{diagnostic odds ratio} by the number of patients enrolled and therefore places designs of
different size, and designs whose size is random, on a common per patient scale.

The starting point was the observation that a single Normal analysis prior offers no protection
against prior misspecification. When the historical data disagree with the new trial, the design
silently loses the error control it was planned under, and its operating characteristics no longer
describe it. This motivated the two ingredients that the rest of the thesis is built on, the robust
MAP prior of \citet{schmidli_robust_2014} and the average operating characteristics of
\citet{best_beyond_2025}, which integrate the conditional quantities over a design prior on the
control parameter rather than evaluating them at a single point.

\section{Methodological Contributions}

\paragraph{An average power}
The formulation of $\avgTIE$ for borrowing on the control arm has a natural counterpart that the
literature does not yet appear to state, namely an average power obtained by integrating the
conditional power over the same design prior. This quantity is the relevant notion of power in this
setting. Classical \textit{assurance} averages over uncertainty in the treatment effect, whereas
under control arm borrowing the uncertainty that matters concerns the control parameter. Together,
$\avgTIE$ and $\avgPow$ are what allows the EUII to be defined in average form.

\paragraph{Closed forms and three properties}
Closed form expressions for $\avgTIE$ and $\avgPow$ were derived under Normal priors, and three
properties of the averaged metrics were established. First, the marginal variance in the denominator
exceeds the sampling variance by the term $(1 - W)^2 \sigma_{D,C}^2$, which accounts for integrating
over $\pi_D$, so average metrics are always pulled closer to $50\%$ than their conditional
counterparts. Second, when the analysis and design priors are aligned the bias term vanishes and the
marginal variance equals the posterior variance, so $\avgTIE$ is controlled exactly at the nominal
level. This restores, in an average sense, the guarantee that borrowing removes. Third, as the design
prior becomes vague the marginal variance diverges and both $\avgTIE$ and $\avgPow$ converge to
$0.5$, whatever the analysis prior, so the metrics degenerate when nothing is assumed about the
control parameter. The frequentist two sample $Z$ test is recovered as $W \to 1$.

\paragraph{Sample size computation}
The closed form for the required control sample size applies only to a single Normal analysis prior.
For mixture priors, and in particular for the MAP prior and its robust extension, no closed form is
available. Since $\avgPow$ is monotone in $n_C$, the smallest adequate sample size can be obtained by
numerical inversion, which is the route taken throughout the applications and the step that makes the
design workflow of Section~\ref{sec:workflow} usable in practice.

\paragraph{Normalization of the index}
Under borrowing it is not obvious whether the patients encoded in the analysis prior should enter the
denominator of the EUII alongside those enrolled in the current trial. We argued that they should
not. The index is a design tool that answers how much evidence each newly enrolled patient
contributes, historical patients are already collected before the trial begins, and adding $n_A$ to
the denominator would conflate the choice of prior with the choice of trial size, penalizing an
informative prior for the very information it supplies. The denominator therefore remains the
quantity the investigator actually controls.

\paragraph{Asymptotic behavior}
The asymptotic value of the EUII was derived under the single Normal model for both the single
criterion and the dual criterion, giving a finite limit as the effect size grows and a reference
against which the finite sample behavior can be read.

\paragraph{A negative result on contrast borrowing}
The framework does not transfer to borrowing on the treatment contrast, and three separate obstacles
were identified. A prior placed on $\delta$ typically favours a beneficial effect and therefore
conflicts with the null, so strict frequentist error control forces the historical information to be
discarded. No exact analogue of the $\avgTIE$ control result is available in that setting. Finally the
EUII itself cannot be built, because the replacement metric available there is a Type III error, a
joint probability whose extra factor reflects only how likely the null was assumed to be, whereas the
\textit{diagnostic odds ratio} requires a conditional false positive rate. The restriction of this
thesis to control arm borrowing is therefore a principled one rather than a matter of convenience.

\section{Findings from the Application}

\paragraph{The robustness weight buys tolerance to drift, and location matters more than spread}
Varying the design prior by switching, by shifting its location, and by scaling its spread showed
that only a control mean better than the one assumed by $\pi_{\text{MAP}}$ can inflate $\avgTIE$, and
that being wrong about where $\theta_C$ lies is considerably more costly than being wrong about how
uncertain it is. Tripling the spread of $\pi_{\text{MAP}}$ inflated $\avgTIE$ less than the mildest
location drift considered. Raising the robustness weight reduced $\avgTIE$ under every form of
conflict. Since $\avgTIE$ is defined for any design prior, this family of sensitivity analyses is
built on a single underlying metric, and the three perturbations reported here do not exhaust it.

\paragraph{The EUII is not a diagnostic for prior misspecification}
This is the clearest negative finding of the applied chapter. Under a conflicting design prior the
EUII curves stayed close to the aligned ones even though the underlying $\avgTIE$ and $\avgPow$ moved
substantially. The reason is structural. The index is a ratio of odds, and a design prior that
disagrees with the analysis prior shifts power and error rate in the same direction, so the two shifts
largely cancel. A design that is badly optimistic and a design that is badly underpowered can
therefore land at nearly the same EUII. The practical recommendation that follows is to assess
sensitivity to prior conflict, and the choice of the robustness weight, through $\avgTIE$ and
$\avgPow$, and to reserve the EUII for comparing design choices under a correctly specified prior,
where differences between designs are attributable to the designs themselves.

\paragraph{The two criteria divide the work rather than dominate one another}
Under aligned priors the EUII curves of the two criteria cross. For effects moderately above the
decision value the \DC has the higher index, and for large effects the \SC does. The mechanism is
that raising the success threshold lowers $\avgTIE$ by an amount that does not depend on the true
effect, since the error rate is evaluated at $\delta = 0$, while the accompanying loss of $\avgPow$
grows with $\delta$. For moderate effects the fixed gain dominates and for large effects the loss
does. The same crossing appears as a function of the control sample size.

\paragraph{Interim analyses raise the information per patient}
Every sequential design considered lay above its fixed counterpart at every effect size, and futility
stopping raised the index further while leaving $\avgTIE$ and $\avgPow$ almost untouched, since a
futility stop removes trials that would have failed at the final analysis anyway. The bands spanning
$\Pr(H_1) \in \{0.01, 0.1, 0.5\}$ were narrow, so the index is not sensitive to that choice.
Futility also reversed the ranking of the two criteria. Without it the \DC had the higher index,
consistent with the fixed design, and with it the \SC overtook the \DC, with the gap widening in
$\delta$.

\paragraph{Comparison at nominal thresholds is not a fair comparison}
Adding a second interim raised the EUII in every case, but at the cost of an inflated $\avgTIE$. The
\DC in particular reached the nominal $5\%$ and lost the conservatism that had justified it in the
fixed design. Designs evaluated at a common threshold therefore do not sit at common error rates, and
an index that rewards evidence per patient will favour the design that is quietly spending more error.
This is what motivates the calibrated comparison.

\section{A Design Workflow}
\label{sec:workflow}
The pieces developed in this thesis compose into a fixed order for planning a new trial in this framework.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Build $\pi_{\text{MAP}}$} from the historical arms. This is an output of the data, not a choice.
    \item \textbf{Choose $\omega$}, fixing the analysis prior $\pi_A$ and, with it, $n_A = \mathrm{ESS}(\pi_A)$.
    \item \textbf{Set the threshold} $p = 1 - \alpha^\ast$. No computation is required: exact under alignment, and, as shown in Section~\ref{subsec:calibrating_omega}, safe under $\pi_D = \pi_{\text{MAP}}$ for every $\omega > 0$.
    \item \textbf{Solve for $n_C$} at the target power under $\pi_D = \pi_{\text{MAP}}$, then $n_T = r_T n_C$.
    \item \textbf{Stress test} $\omega$ against a declared \textit{drift}, by shifting $\pi_{\text{MAP}}$. If the worst case fails the stated tolerance, raise $\omega$ and return to step 3.
\end{enumerate}
Steps 3 and 4 are coupled whenever the analysis prior is a mixture, since $\avgTIE$ then depends on $n_C$ and no closed form is available, and are solved jointly by the numerical inversion of Section~\ref{sec:fixed_design}. Step 5 is where the robust component of $\pi_A$ earns its keep, which is why it belongs there and not in $\pi_D$.

The same order applies to a sequential design, with one caveat. No closed form is available for the
average sequential operating characteristics, so every evaluation in steps 3 and 4 rests on Monte
Carlo simulation, and the threshold has to be recalibrated at each candidate sample size rather than
read off a formula.

\section{Limitations}

The scope of the work is deliberately narrow in several respects. The endpoint is Normal with a known
standard deviation, and the consequences of misspecifying that standard deviation were not
investigated. Borrowing is on the control arm only, for the reasons set out above. The analysis
priors considered are the MAP prior and its robust extension, so \textit{power priors} and
\textit{commensurate priors} were not evaluated on the same footing.

Two limitations concern the index itself. It is uninformative under prior misspecification, as
established above, so it cannot serve as a general purpose summary of a design and must be paired with
the average operating characteristics. Its sequential form also requires a prior probability
$\Pr(H_1)$ that the treatment works, and although the bands reported here were narrow, that input has
no counterpart in the fixed design and is a judgement the investigator must supply.

Finally, Bayesian group sequential designs with robust borrowing remain rare in practice. A scoping
review of Phase II trials found that the parameters governing the degree of borrowing are poorly
reported, with only a third of trials applying a fixed downweighting explaining how the weight was
chosen \citep{weru2026uptake}. The methods developed here therefore address a setting that is
currently underused, and the conclusions have not been tested against practice.

\section{Future Work}

\note{TO DO: revisit once the calibrated comparison is written, since the most natural extensions
depend on what it shows.}

Several directions follow naturally. The sensitivity analyses could be extended beyond the three
perturbations reported here, for instance by reweighting the mixture components of $\pi_{\text{MAP}}$
or by refitting it with one historical study left out at a time. The timing of the interim analysis
was held fixed throughout and could be optimized against the index. Finally, the practical
consequences for the motivating trial deserve a direct statement, namely which of the designs
considered should be recommended for a proof of concept study of this kind and what would have been
gained over the original protocol.
